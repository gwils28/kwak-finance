## 1. Purpose

A self-hosted web application to manage a household's **budget** (cash flows), **net worth** (assets and liabilities) and **financial goals** (amounts to gather by a date), designed so that analytics, data-science and local generative-AI features can be added in later phases without reworking the core.

The project doubles as a reference implementation of the maintainer's Claude Code development practices (see `docs/CLAUDE_CODE.md`).

## 2. Scope and constraints

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Users | One household: a few members sharing the spending, each with private wealth lines and goals if they wish. Beside it, a demo household of fictitious data (F-ADM-5), which nobody signs in to |
| Hosting | Self-hosted only (Docker Compose on a local machine / home server). No cloud dependency |
| Currency | **EUR only** for spending lines, income, budgets and wealth values |
| Data sources | Manual entry only: spending and income lines as they come (weekly or monthly), wealth values monthly. No bank file import (since v1.0) and no market-data API |
| Out of scope | Bank aggregation APIs (DSP2), payments, tax filing, multi-household SaaS, native mobile app |
| Language | Code, docs, UI and commits in English |

## 3. Users and roles

- **Owner**: creates the household, manages members, backups and settings.
- **Member**: full access to the spending (lines, income, budget, shared by the whole household), to shared and joint wealth lines and goals, and to their own private ones.
- The spending has no visibility: every member sees and edits every line (since v1.0). Visibility is per wealth line and per goal: `private` (owner only), `shared` (all household members, still the owner's) or `joint` (the household's common holding: all members, nobody's own). The wealth and the goals let each user choose *household* (everything visible), *joint* (the joint lines and goals) or *mine* (their own, joint ones excluded).

## 4. Functional requirements

Priority: **M** = MVP, **S** = should (later in MVP), **L** = later phase.

### 4.1 Authentication and household (M)

- F-AUTH-1: Login with email + password (argon2id hashing), server-side session in an httpOnly cookie.
- F-AUTH-2: Mandatory TOTP 2FA, with one-time recovery codes.
- F-AUTH-3: Owner invites members (invite link, single use, expiring).
- F-AUTH-4: Login rate limiting and session revocation.

### 4.2 Spending lines and income (M)

Since v1.0 the spending side has no accounts, no bank import and no transfers: the maintainer enters every expense by hand, once a week or once a month, and a statement only got in the way (rows to sort again, duplicates of what was already entered). An expense is **one line**, whichever account or card paid it.

- F-LIN-1: A **line** has a date, a label (1 to 100 characters), an amount to the cent and a category (F-CAT-1), required: a line is never left "to categorise". It records who entered it and when. Spending belongs to the household: every member sees and edits every line.
- F-LIN-2: A line counts in the **month of its date**, in every figure. There is no budget month: a salary paid on the 28th is the income of the month of its date, unless the line is dated otherwise (F-INC-1).
- F-LIN-3: An **expense** line is an outflow, entered as a positive amount (stored negative, §6). A **savings** line (F-CAT-4) is money put aside, entered as a positive amount, or money taken back from savings, entered with a minus sign. An **income** line (F-CAT-1) is money received, entered as a positive amount (stored positive); refunds (health insurance, a returned item) are income lines. A line is income or spending, never both: a split line's parts are all income or none (F-LIN-4).
- F-LIN-4: **Split**: a line can be spread over two or more **parts**, each a category and a positive amount. The user enters every part but the last, which takes the rest, so the parts always add up exactly to the line (invariant 2). A split line counts in each part's category, for its part's amount, in every figure by category; the grid shows it once, as "Split" with its parts. When the amount of a split line changes, the last part takes the difference; the change is refused if that part would reach zero or less. Removing the split leaves the line in its first part's category.
- F-LIN-5: Deleting a line can be undone straight away, from the message that confirms it; it is recorded in the activity log (F-ADM-3).
- F-INC-1: **Monthly income** (since v1.3): a month's income is the sum of its **income lines** (F-LIN-3), in the income categories (salaries, benefits, refunds…). A month without income lines has none; nothing is carried from the month before. The single monthly amount of v1.0 was turned into one income line on the first of each month, in Other income, from the first amount entered to the month of the migration, the carried months included.

### 4.3 Entering lines (M)

Entry is made for a session at a computer, keyboard first: every action below is reachable without the mouse.

- F-ENT-1: **The grid**, the Spending space's main page: one month (the current one by default, the previous and next ones a key away), its lines sorted by date then by entry order, every cell editable in place. An **empty row** is always ready at the bottom: date, label, amount, category; **Tab** moves to the next cell, **Enter** saves the row and opens a new one with the same date, **Escape** cancels the row's changes. A row is saved only once complete. A new row's date is the previous row's in the session, otherwise today (for a past month, its last day). The header shows the month's income (its income lines, F-INC-1), spent, saved and the rest (F-DSH-4).
- F-ENT-2: **Quick line**: a text field above the grid takes a line written **in any order**, e.g. `boulangerie 12,50 hier`, `45 sncf 13/10` or `carrefour 87.40 #foyer`:
  - a **date** is a day word (*today*, *yesterday*, *the day before yesterday*), a weekday (the latest such day up to today) or a `dd/mm` or `dd/mm/yyyy` date, in the user's language; without one, the date follows F-ENT-1;
  - the **amount** is the last number left (comma or dot decimals, an optional `€`, a leading `-` for a savings withdrawal), stored positive when the category is an income one (F-LIN-3); the other numbers stay in the label;
  - `#word` **forces the category** whose name starts with that word (case and accents ignored), when exactly one does;
  - the rest is the **label**, whose category comes from the label memory (F-ENT-3).
  Below the field, the line is shown as understood while it is typed. Enter saves it when it is complete; otherwise it goes into the grid's empty row, the missing cell focused.
- F-ENT-3: **Label memory**: the label cell and the quick line suggest the labels already used in the household that start with what is typed (case and accents ignored), the most used over the last 12 months first. Choosing a known label, or typing one exactly, fills in the **category** of the latest line with that label, never the amount; the user may change it, and the latest choice is the one remembered. There are no categorisation rules.
- F-ENT-4: **Where I stopped**: the line entered last (by entry time) is marked in the grid, and the header says its date and when it was entered ("last entry: Tue 15/10, entered on 16/10"), with a link to its month when it is in another one.

### 4.4 Categories (M)

- F-CAT-1: **One level** of categories (since v1.0), each with a **kind**: **expense**, **savings** or **income** (since v1.3). The default set, in the owner's language: Housing (Logement), Groceries (Courses), Transport, Children (Enfants), Health (Santé), Subscriptions (Abonnements), Home (Foyer), Leisure (Loisirs), Shopping, Gifts (Cadeaux), Miscellaneous (Divers), Savings (Épargne), the one savings category, and four income categories: Salaries (Salaires), Benefits (Aides et allocations), Refunds (Remboursements) and Other income (Autres revenus). An income category has no target (F-BUD-1) and no fixed expense (F-FIX-1); the budget and the reports show what it received. Categories can be renamed, added and deleted; deleting a category that holds lines, split parts, fixed expenses or targets asks for another category of the same kind to move them to. "Restore defaults" adds back the default categories that are missing.
- F-CAT-4: A **savings** category holds the money put aside (a payment into a savings account, an investment): its lines are **saved**, never spending, and reduce the month's rest (F-DSH-4); a withdrawal counts against them. Savings are valued in the wealth matrix (§4.7), which reads nothing from these lines.
- F-CAT-6: **One-off** expense categories, paid once in the month (rent, a subscription), so their spending is complete from the day they are paid and is never judged against the pace of the month nor the warning threshold (F-BUD-2). Off by default; the default set marks Housing and Subscriptions as one-off.

### 4.5 Budgets (M)

Goal of the budget module: **compare spending per category with a target** set beforehand, month by month and over the year, with nothing to sort first.

- F-BUD-1: A **monthly target** per expense category, set **from a month on** (this month by default) and kept until it is changed: a change from a month leaves the earlier months' targets as they were, and removing a target leaves the category without one from that month. Setting a target from a past month says which months change.
- F-BUD-9: A **savings objective** per savings category, a monthly minimum set like a target (F-BUD-1): saving more is good. Its status mirrors a target's with the band and colours of F-BUD-6: **ahead** (saved more than the band above the objective) in green, **on** in neutral, **behind** (more than the band below) in burnt orange.
- F-BUD-2: **The month so far**, a section of the overview (F-DSH-1) for the month it shows (default: the current one): one line per expense category with a target that month, ranked by share used, highest first: spent, target, remaining (or the overrun), **share used** (spent ÷ target) and a progress bar. Categories without a target are not listed, nor savings categories, which have their own section below. Each line has a status, shown with an icon and a word as well as a colour:
  - **on track**: share used below the warning threshold;
  - **watch**: from the threshold up to 100 %, except for a **one-off** category (F-CAT-6), which is on track up to 100 %;
  - **over**: above 100 %.
  The **warning threshold** is a household setting (default 80 %, from 50 % to 100 %), changed by the owner. For the current month, a **pace** marker on each bar shows the share of the month elapsed (today's day ÷ the month's number of days); it is not shown on a one-off category nor for a past month. A line opens the month's grid filtered on the category.
  Below it, a second section, **the month's savings objectives**, lists each savings category with an objective (F-BUD-9) that month, the least advanced first (lowest saved ÷ objective): saved, objective, what is left to save (or how far beyond), the share of the objective and a bar. Its status is **reached** (saved ≥ objective: ✓ and a green bar) or **in progress** (a lighter green bar), with no band: unlike the matrix's ahead / on / behind (F-BUD-9), which judges a month once its savings are made, the month so far only says whether the objective is met yet; it has no warning threshold and no pace, since saving is often done in one go early in the month. The two sections are titled *Targets this month: spending budget* and *Targets this month: savings*.
- F-BUD-6: **Budget matrix**: one row per expense category, one column per month (default: the last 12), plus a first column with the category's current target. The matrix scrolls within its frame: the month headers stay at the top and the category and target columns on the left. Each cell shows the month's spending, its **gap to that month's target in € and in %** ((spent − target) / target), and a colour:
  - **under**: spent more than 5 % below the target → green;
  - **on target**: within ±5 % → neutral (pale grey);
  - **over**: more than 5 % above the target → burnt orange / terracotta.
  The 5 % band is a setting. A month without a target shows its spending without a colour. Above the expenses, an **income** section shows what each income category received, without target nor colour; below the expenses, a **savings** section holds the savings categories against their objectives (F-BUD-9); then rows for the month's **income**, **spent**, **saved** and **rest**. Clicking a cell opens that month's grid filtered on the category.
- F-BUD-10: **The year**: for a calendar year (the current one by default), per category: the targets **to date** (the targets of January to this month added up, the current month in full, so a running month leans under) and for the **full year**, the spending to date, the gap to date in € and in % with the status of F-BUD-6, and the number of months over, on and under their target; the same for the savings objectives, what each income category received to date, then the year's income, spending, savings, rest and savings rate. A past year is shown whole.

### 4.6 Fixed expenses (M)

Rent, the childminder, subscriptions and insurance come back every period: fixed expenses save typing them and say what is still to come.

- F-FIX-1: A **fixed expense** has a label, a category (expense or savings, e.g. a monthly payment into a savings account; never income), an amount, a **frequency** (monthly, quarterly or yearly), a day of the month (the last day standing in for a missing one), a first month and optionally a last month; it can be paused. Fixed expenses are listed, added and changed on their own page, opened from the grid.
- F-FIX-2: **To confirm**: each occurrence due in a month (every month from the first one, every third month, or every twelfth) is shown at the top of that month's grid, greyed, with its date. **Confirming** it (Enter on the row, or its button) creates the line with the fixed expense's label, category, amount and date, each of which can be changed first; an occurrence can also be **skipped** for the month, which can be undone. An occurrence counts as confirmed as soon as its month holds a line created from it, or a line typed by hand with the same label (case and accents ignored) and category that no other occurrence took. Nothing is ever created by itself.
- F-FIX-3: A fixed expense's **monthly equivalent** is its amount for a monthly one, a third of it for a quarterly one and a twelfth for a yearly one (used by F-GOAL-2).
- F-FIX-4: The overview (F-DSH-1) lists the current month's occurrences still to confirm, with "Confirm", and the **projected rest** at the end of the month: the rest (F-DSH-4) minus the occurrences still to confirm.

### 4.7 Wealth / net worth (M)

Net worth is followed as a **monthly wealth matrix**, like the maintainer's own spreadsheet: what each holding is worth at the end of each month, by category. There are no securities positions, no market prices and no loan schedules: every value is entered by hand once a month, the bank accounts' too, which is what the maintainer already does.

- F-WLT-1: **Wealth categories**, two levels. Defaults, in the owner's language: Savings accounts (Livrets), Current accounts (Comptes courants), Stocks (Actions), Crypto (Cryptos), Bonds (Obligations), Private equity, Gold (Or), Real estate (Immobilier), Equipment (Matériels), Exotic (Exotiques) and **Debts** (Dettes), the one liability category. Categories can be renamed, recoloured, moved, added (an asset or a debt) or deleted, from "Manage categories" on the wealth page: a category holding lines cannot be (any line counts, a member's private ones included, which would be lost otherwise). Each category has its own colour from the `wealth-*` set (§7); its lines share it.
- F-WLT-2: **Wealth lines** are the subcategories: one holding each (e.g. "PEA Fortuneo" and "CTO Trade Republic" under Stocks, "Lynxéa Spirit 2" where the owner files it, "Crédit immobilier" under Debts). A line has an **owner** and a **visibility** (`private`, `shared` or `joint`, §3); a private line is shown to its owner only. A line can be closed from a month on (sold, repaid): it keeps its history and leaves later months. A Savings-accounts line may have a **regulated type** (Livret A, LDDS, LEP, PEL, Livret jeune; none by default), whose ceiling F-ANA-8 watches (R6); these accounts are a person's own, so a joint line cannot have one.
- F-WLT-3: **Monthly values**: each line has at most one value per calendar month, in EUR, positive (a debt is entered as what is still owed and counts negatively). A month without a value carries the previous one forward, shown as carried, until a new value is entered; before its first value a line is empty. A closed month takes no value, and the matrix ends with the current month at the latest: later months are unknown. Values are entered in the matrix or with **"Update this month"**, which lists the open lines with their last value pre-filled and saves them all at once, or none if one is refused.
- F-WLT-4: **Bank accounts are lines entered by hand** (since v0.16): Savings accounts and Current accounts are ordinary categories, where the user adds one line per account ("Livret A", "Compte joint"…) and enters its value each month like any other line. Nothing is read from the spending (F-CAT-4): the matrix shows what the maintainer enters.
- F-WLT-5: **The wealth matrix**: lines grouped by category in rows, months in columns (the last 12 by default, from the **wealth start** at the earliest; any other period of up to 120 months can be chosen, ending this month at the latest), with category subtotals in the category's colour, **gross assets**, **debts** and **net worth**, and for each month the change against the previous one (€ and %). A column gives each category's **share** of gross assets for the last month shown. Scope: *household*, *joint* or *mine* (§3). As the budget matrix (F-BUD-6), it scrolls within its frame, its month headers and its line names staying in view both ways.
- F-WLT-6: **Wealth charts**, on the wealth's *Analysis* page, over the months and scope chosen there (as F-WLT-5), below the same summary tiles, each category in its colour:
  - **net worth over time**: each category's total per month, stacked (debts below zero), with net worth as a line;
  - **allocation** of a chosen month (the last one shown by default, one choice for both charts) as a **sunburst**: categories in the inner ring, their lines in the outer one, gross assets in the centre;
  - the same allocation as a **mosaic** (a squarified treemap, as Finary shows it): one tile per category, as large as its value, the largest first, labelled with its name, value and share when it has the room;
  - **what moved net worth**: a bridge from the first month's net worth to the last's, one step per category that moved (a debt repaid lifts it);
  - the net worth tile adds its change over the months shown, the debts tile the **debt ratio** (debts / gross assets).
  Every chart has its table, and the sunburst and the mosaic read the pointed or focused mark out in words: no value is reachable only by hovering.
- F-WLT-7: The overview (F-DSH-1) opens with net worth at the end of its month and its change over that month, in its scope, once the wealth has a line, with a link to the wealth page.

### 4.8 Dashboard and reporting (M)

- F-DSH-1: Home page showing net worth with its change, the month's figures (F-DSH-4), the month so far (F-BUD-2), the fixed expenses still to confirm with the projected rest (F-FIX-4), the goals (F-GOAL-9) and the last lines entered.
- F-DSH-4: Spending KPIs for any month (default: current): **income** (income lines, F-INC-1), **spent** (expense lines), **saved** (savings lines, net of withdrawals), the **rest** (income − spent − saved) and the **savings rate** ((income − spent) / income); change vs the previous month and vs the 12-month average; top 5 categories.
- F-DSH-5: Spending charts: by category (bar chart); monthly evolution over 12 months (stacked bars by category, income line); cumulative spending within the month against the sum of its targets.
- F-DSH-2: **Reports**: income, spending and savings by category over any period of whole months (presets: this month, last month, this quarter, this year, last year, the last 12 months; or any first and last month, 10 years at most). A period running past the current month stops there ("this year" means this year so far) and is compared with the same months of the period before, so monthly averages and changes compare like with like. A change is measured against the size of the previous figure: a rest going from −500 € to +200 € is +140 %. Months before the first line entered are left out. A report shows:
  - the period's **totals**: income, spent, saved, the rest and the savings rate, each against the **previous period of the same length** (a year against the year before, a quarter against the quarter before);
  - a **table by category** in three sections (income, spending, savings): the total, the monthly average, the share of its section and the change against the previous period;
  - a **monthly chart**: income and spending per month, with the rest;
  - the **money flows** (Sankey): the income by category on the left, a central "available" node, then spending by category, savings and the **unallocated** rest on the right; a shortfall comes in on the left as a deficit, money taken back from savings as such. Both sides add up to the same total. The spending of a category over its targets for the period is burnt orange, within them (or without a target) ochre.

### 4.9 Administration (M)

- F-ADM-1: Automatic daily backup (`pg_dump`) with a retention policy (e.g. 7 daily, 4 weekly, 12 monthly) to a configurable directory.
- F-ADM-2: A documented restore procedure, plus a restore test script run regularly.
- F-ADM-3: **Activity log** of sensitive actions, written in the same database transaction as the action itself:
  - **sign-ins**: completed (with the second factor used: authenticator code or recovery code), failed on a known account (wrong password or wrong code), sign-outs, recovery codes renewed. They keep the client's address;
  - **household**: invites created, revoked or accepted, settings changed;
  - **deletions**: lines, categories (with the category their lines moved to), fixed expenses and goals (F-GOAL-8);
  - the default categories restored.

  Each entry records who, when and what, with a short description frozen at the time (label, amount, count), so it still reads after the object is gone. The **owner** sees the whole household's log, a **member** only their own entries; an entry about a private goal or wealth line is shown only to its owner. The log is read-only in the app and can be filtered by kind and, for the owner, by member. Attempts refused by the sign-in limit (F-AUTH-4) are not logged, the failures that reached it are.
- F-ADM-4: **Wealth start**: the owner may set the first month the wealth matrix opens from (F-WLT-5), never a month to come; without one, nothing limits it.
- F-ADM-5: **Demo**: a demo household of fictitious data, deep enough to try every feature before the real data has a history.
  - **Opening**: any signed-in member opens it from *Demo*, next to *Activity log* in the account menu. Their session then acts as the demo's owner until they leave: every page, report, analysis and Dash page shows the demo household, under a banner "You are in the demo" with *Back to my household*. Signing out leaves the demo too. The session is still the member's: it is revoked, expires and is limited as theirs (F-AUTH-4).
  - **Isolation**: the demo is a household of its own, marked as the demo, with its own members, categories, lines, values, goals and settings, and its own activity log; nothing in it is linked to the real household, which never sees it in any total, scope, log or analysis. The demo's members are fictitious: they have no password nor second factor and nobody signs in as them. In the demo, the invites, the members' management, the password, the second factor, the recovery codes and the sessions are out of reach (the menu hides them and the API refuses them); everything else works and saves as in the real household.
  - **Content**, generated from a fixed seed and dated from the day it is made: a couple and a child (the child has no account), about 36 months of history up to today. Spending: monthly net salaries (a raise, a yearly bonus), every default category, fixed expenses confirmed, skipped or still to confirm this month, split lines, a few one-off months (holidays, a car repair), monthly targets that change over time, so the matrix, the month so far, the year and the reports are all filled. Wealth: current and savings accounts (regulated ones among them), a life insurance, a stock account, the home with its mortgage and a consumer loan, valued every month, with private, shared and joint lines for both members and a wealth start. Goals: a precaution, short, medium and long-term goals funded by these lines (one reached, one late, one closed), a locked pleasure goal and an unlocked one. Projection and retirement: assumptions by category and both members' birth, retirement month, earnings and pension. Some points to look at (F-ANA-7) show up. The amounts are plausible for a French household, never anyone's real figures.
  - **Kept**: what a member changes in the demo stays, for every member, until a reset. *Reset the demo*, on the banner, after a confirmation, deletes the whole demo household and generates it again, dated from that day. Nothing else touches it; backups include it like the rest of the database.
  - **Created on first use**: the demo is generated the first time a member opens it, not at install, and never by a migration.

### 4.10 Financial goals (phase 4)

A **goal** is an amount to gather by a date: an emergency fund, a trip, a home deposit. It has nothing to do with the budget's monthly targets (F-BUD-1) or savings objectives (F-BUD-9), which judge a month's flows. Goals are arranged as a **pyramid** of three tiers on a **foundation**, the emergency fund, under a golden **capstone** for pleasure goals (F-GOAL-10), and funded by **wealth lines** (F-WLT-2), so their progress follows the values entered each month in the wealth matrix, with nothing more to enter.

- F-GOAL-1: **The pyramid**: its **foundation** is the precaution (below); above it, three **tiers**, from the base, given by the deadline's distance from this month, never chosen: **long term** (beyond 5 years: a home deposit, the children's studies), **medium term** (over 12 months, up to 5 years: a trip, a large purchase) and, at the top, **short term** (up to 12 months: holidays, a repair). A goal **moves tier by itself** when its deadline changes or, as months pass, when it comes within a nearer tier's horizon, climbing towards the top. A goal may be **permanent**: it stays in view once its target is reached (any other goal is then archived by itself, F-GOAL-8), and its deadline is optional (without one, it is in the short term, and its status is **open** rather than ahead or behind). A goal may be the **precaution**, the emergency fund, filled first (F-GOAL-6), always permanent and in the foundation, whatever its deadline. A goal may instead be a **pleasure** goal, in the capstone (F-GOAL-10). A goal has a name, a **target** amount (EUR, positive), a **deadline** month (required but for a permanent goal, none for a pleasure goal, never a past month), a **start** month (the month it is created by default, or an earlier one, from the wealth start, to count progress already made), an **owner** and a **visibility** (`private`, `shared` or `joint`, as a line, §3). Each member has at most one open precaution goal, and the household at most one joint one.
- F-GOAL-2: **The precaution's target** is a fixed amount. The form proposes one: a number of months (6 by default, from 1 to 12) of the **average monthly fixed expenses**, the sum of the monthly equivalents (F-FIX-3) of the fixed expenses in an expense category not paused nor ended, shown with its detail; the spending being the household's, the proposal is the same in every scope. Accepting it copies the amount into the target, which never changes by itself afterwards. Without a fixed expense there is no proposal, but a link to the fixed expenses page.
- F-GOAL-3: **Funding** by **envelopes**: a goal is funded by asset lines (never a debt), each holding an envelope, the part of the line set aside for it; the rest of the line is **free**, for anything else. A funding has an **opening amount** (in euros, 0 by default, taken from the line's free part in the goal's start month) and a **share of the rises** (1 to 100 %, 100 % by default); a line's shares of the rises sum to 100 % at most across open goals. Month by month from the goal's start, with only the lines' values entered (F-WLT-3, carried forward, nothing after a line's closing): when a line **rises** (a deposit, interest, a gain), each goal it funds receives its share of the rise, never beyond its target, nor a locked pleasure goal (F-GOAL-10), the rest staying free; when a line **falls** (a withdrawal, a loss), the fall comes out of the **free part first**, and its goals only lose once the line is worth less than they hold, cut from the pleasure goals first, then from the farthest deadline to the nearest and the precaution last, so they never hold more than the line. When the app credits a goal with a rise that was not for it (a deposit for something else), its envelope can be **adjusted** by hand from a month: from then on it holds the amount set (never more than the line's free part plus what it held, nor beyond its target), and goes on rising and falling as before; an adjustment can be removed. An archived or cancelled goal frees its envelopes. A goal's **value** in a month is the sum of its envelopes. Changing an opening amount or a share recomputes the goal from its start, which the form says. The goal form shows each line's free part, and the Wealth page each line's part set aside for goals; both count only the goals the viewer sees. A goal uses its owner's lines and joint ones; a joint goal joint lines only; a private line funds only a private goal, whose value would otherwise show it, and cannot turn private while it funds another.
- F-GOAL-4: **Monthly tracking and status**: for each month from the start, the goal's value, its **progress** (value ÷ target) and what it gained over the month. The **expected** value follows a straight line from the value of the start month to the target at the deadline. The status uses the band of the budget (F-BUD-6): **reached** (the value meets the target), **ahead** (above the expected value by more than the band), **on track** (within it) or **behind** (below it by more than the band), in green, neutral and burnt orange as a savings objective (F-BUD-9). A precaution goal short of its target is **to fill first** instead, in ochre; the other short-term goals have the statuses above. A pleasure goal short of its target is **locked**, behind a padlock, or **open** (F-GOAL-10). Every status has its icon and word.
- F-GOAL-5: **Estimates**: the amount still to gather; for a goal with a deadline, the months left and the **required effort** (what is left ÷ the months left, at least one); the **actual effort**, the average monthly gain over the last 6 months (fewer for a younger goal; with no complete month yet: "not enough history"); the **estimated date** (this month plus what is left ÷ the actual effort, rounded up), or "never at this pace" when the actual effort is nil or negative; against the deadline, how many months early or late, and when late, the extra effort per month that keeps the date (required − actual).
- F-GOAL-6: **The month's effort plan**, a recommendation (nothing is moved): the **capacity** is the household's average monthly income minus spending (F-DSH-4, savings lines left in, since they are what goals are funded from) over the last 6 complete months from the first one with a line, the same in every scope, never below zero, unless the owner sets it by hand (a household setting). While the scope's precaution goals fall short, they receive their gap, up to a **precaution share** of the capacity (a household setting, from 50 % to 100 %, 70 % by default), split by gap; the rest goes to the other open goals not yet reached, short-term ones included, in proportion to their required effort and never beyond it; what they leave goes to the precaution until its gap is filled, then to the pleasure goals that are not locked, in proportion to what each still lacks and never beyond it (F-GOAL-10), then is shown as **unallocated**. The plan says in how many months the precaution is filled at this pace and how much the other goals fall behind each month until then.
- F-GOAL-7: **The Goals page**, a space of its own (§7, its *Pyramid* tab, next to *Analysis*, §4.12), in the *household*, *joint* or *mine* scope (§3): the **pyramid**, its foundation, each tier and the capstone filled by its goals' values over their targets and labelled with the percentage; while a precaution goal falls short, a warning with the missing amount; the effort plan (F-GOAL-6); then each tier with its goals as cards: a **gauge** with a **pace** marker at the expected value, the value and target, the progress, the status, the deadline, the estimated date, the required and actual efforts, and a sentence ("at this pace, 6 months after the deadline: +€80 a month keeps the date"), and its actions: edit, archive, cancel (F-GOAL-8). A card opens the goal's page: the estimates as tiles, a **trajectory** chart (the values, the expected line, the projection at the actual effort, the target and the deadline) and its table by month (value, gained, expected, gap), the latest first, and its envelopes, line by line, with their opening amount, share of the rises, what they hold and their adjustments (F-GOAL-3).
- F-GOAL-8: **Life cycle**: a goal can be edited, from its card or its page, then **archived** (done with, kept for the record) or **cancelled** (no longer wanted), after this month on its card or from a chosen month on its page; a goal that is not permanent is **archived by itself** after the month it reaches its target, and reopening it asks for a higher target or for it to become permanent; a permanent goal can be cancelled, not archived. An archived or cancelled goal leaves the pyramid, its lines' shares are freed, and it stays readable but **hidden by default** on the Goals page, which can show the archived and cancelled goals; one can be reopened. Deleting a goal removes it and its funding, never a line.
- F-GOAL-9: The overview (F-DSH-1) shows, once a goal exists, the progress of the foundation, the three tiers and the capstone, and the precaution warning, with a link to the Goals page.
- F-GOAL-10: **The capstone**, the pyramid's golden tip, holds the **pleasure goals**: things wanted only for the pleasure of it, not needed (a pair of boxing gloves, a record player, a fine bottle of rum). A pleasure goal has **no deadline**, is never the precaution nor permanent (it is archived by itself once reached, F-GOAL-8), and is funded like any goal (F-GOAL-3), but only once the essentials are in order: it is **locked** in a month unless, at the end of the month before, in its scope (its owner's goals and the joint ones; the joint ones only for a joint goal): there is a precaution goal and **every precaution goal is reached**, and **at least three quarters** of the open goals with a deadline are **ahead** or reached (on track or behind do not count; permanent goals without a deadline are left out; with no such goal, only the precaution counts). While locked, the line rises it would have received stay free, the effort plan gives it nothing, and what it already holds stays; its opening amount and its adjustments by hand, set on purpose, still apply. Once unlocked, its status is **open**. Its estimates are those of a goal without a deadline (F-GOAL-5): what is left, the actual effort and the estimated date. The capstone shows when it is locked, with a padlock, the word and why ("precaution at 82 %", "2 of 4 goals ahead, 3 needed").

### 4.11 Wealth projection (phase 5)

Where the wealth could be in 1 to 40 years, given what it holds today, what the household saves each month and what each kind of holding may earn: a **Monte Carlo** simulation, drawn as a fan of outcomes rather than one line, since returns are uncertain. It is an estimate, never a forecast nor advice, and the page says so. The spending itself is forecast apart, from its own history (§4.14); retirement comes next, on the same engine (§4.13).

- F-SIM-1: **Assumptions**, a household setting any member can change, kept until changed: for each wealth category, an expected **annual return**, the arithmetic mean of the yearly return (negative for something that loses value), and a **volatility**, its standard deviation (0 for no uncertainty); an annual **inflation** rate (2 % by default); and for each debt line, its **monthly payment** (borrower insurance excluded), its **annual interest rate** (the nominal rate, not the APR) and its **borrower insurance** a month, which repays nothing. Defaults for the default categories (F-WLT-1), nominal, per year: Savings accounts 1.7 % ± 0 (the Livret A and LDDS rate since 1 August 2026; a LEP earns 2.5 %, an assurance-vie euro fund about 2.5 % before social charges: set your own), Current accounts 0 % ± 0, Stocks 7 % ± 16 %, Crypto 8 % ± 60 %, Bonds 3 % ± 6 %, Private equity 6 % ± 25 % (net of fees; retail funds charge 3 to 4 % a year), Gold 3 % ± 15 %, Real estate 2.5 % ± 8 % (the property's value before selling costs, rents not counted: they are income lines), Equipment −15 % ± 0 (what it loses each year), Exotic 0 % ± 20 %; a category added later starts at 0 % ± 0. A debt line starts with no payment set: it stays as owed until one is. Returns are **after the fees of a low-cost fund** (about 0.3 % a year) and **before tax**: the projected wealth is what the holdings would be worth, not what selling them would bring after tax on the gains (18.6 % social charges on a PEA over 5 years, 31.4 % on a CTO, 17.2 % plus income tax on an assurance vie, livrets tax-free); a contract charging more calls for a lower return. The form shows each assumption with its default, which it can restore, and next to each category its **median annual growth** implied by its return and volatility (Stocks 7 % ± 16 % grow by 5.8 % a year in the median outcome, Crypto 8 % ± 60 % lose 5.6 %), since the median path grows slower than the mean. Returns, volatilities and the inflation are set to 0.1 % at most, a debt's rate to 0.01 % (as loans are quoted): a return above −100 % and up to +100 % a year, a volatility from 0 to 200 %, an interest rate from 0 to 100 %, the inflation from −5 % to 20 %. A payment at or below the month's interest lets the debt grow: the form warns of it.
- F-SIM-2: **The starting point** is the current month of the wealth matrix (F-WLT-3, values carried forward), in the scope chosen as on the wealth page (*household*, *joint* or *mine*, §3, the viewer's private lines in *mine* and *household* only): each asset category's total and each debt line's amount. Nothing after it is read. Without a wealth line in scope, the page asks to enter the wealth first.
- F-SIM-3: **Monthly savings** are the household's **monthly contributions** when it has any: a list of what it invests each month, **at most one per asset category** (a debt takes none), each an amount from €1 to €100,000 a month that either **follows inflation** (the default, as an income keeping pace with prices: €500 today is €743 a month in 20 years at 2 %) or stays a **fixed amount** in current euros (as a standing order usually does: the page says to untick it then), a household setting any member can change, kept until changed. Their **sum is the monthly savings** and their **split the allocation** (each contribution over the sum), so a contribution goes to its category only; the return and volatility it earns are its category's (F-SIM-1), the same for what the category already holds, and changing a contribution's return changes the category's assumption, which the retirement page and the goals analysis read too: saving says so. Contributions to Savings accounts meet the ceilings the projection does not model: when the median of Savings accounts at the horizon, in current euros (the ceilings are not indexed), is above €34,950 a member in scope (a Livret A and an LDDS full), the page says that beyond them the 1.7 % rate is not available and that a lower return suits this contribution. The page compares the sum with the goals' capacity (F-GOAL-6): "N % of your measured capacity (€X a month)", and above 100 % asks whether it can be kept up, since what is not saved cannot be invested; the capacity's warnings below (fewer than 12 months) still show. Without any contribution, the monthly savings are the goals' capacity, split by the default allocation below, as before. The contributions are the simulator's: the retirement keeps the capacity (F-RET-3). In the *mine* and *joint* scopes, the contributions are the household's, and the page says they may be lowered. With contributions, the savings are never negative. Without them, the page says over how many months the capacity was measured, and that fewer than 12 miss the year's irregular costs (taxes, insurance, holidays); in the *mine* and *joint* scopes, that it is the whole household's and may be lowered. Savings are in **today's euros**: in current euros they grow with the inflation each month, as an income keeping pace with prices. They are added each month, split across the asset categories by an **allocation**: the contributions' split, else by default the categories' shares of today's gross assets, Current accounts, Real estate, Equipment, Private equity and Exotic left out (one keeps a working balance on a current account, and monthly savings buy neither a home, equipment, locked-in private equity nor wine, art or watches unless an allocation says so); a scenario may set its own allocation, in percentages summing to 100. With nothing left to share by (only current accounts, real estate, equipment, private equity or exotic holdings), the savings go to Savings accounts, else to Current accounts; without either category, the page asks for an allocation. The holdings are **never rebalanced**: the categories that grow fastest take a growing share. **Negative savings** (spending more than the income) are taken from Current accounts, then Savings accounts, then the other categories in proportion to what they hold, Real estate, Equipment, Private equity and Exotic left out (the illiquid categories of F-ANA-1: a home, things to resell, locked-in funds, wine or art are not sold to pay the month's spending); once nothing of these is left, the missing amount builds up as a debt, **Shortfall**, borrowed at 8 % a year (an overdraft or a consumer loan costs several percent, up to the usury rate, about 20 %), which later savings repay first. The debts are repaid apart from the savings, since their payments already are spending lines: each month a debt line accrues its interest (its rate / 12 × what is owed) and falls by its payment, in current euros, until it reaches zero (with no rate, the payment is all capital); what its payment no longer needs that month, then all of it from the month after, with its insurance, which stops with the loan, **joins the monthly savings**, split by the same allocation. A debt already repaid at the start frees nothing: its payment is no longer spent, so the capacity already counts it.
- F-SIM-4: **The simulation**, month by month over the horizon (20 years by default, 1 to 40): each asset category first grows by a random return, drawn as a monthly log-return from a normal whose parameters make twelve months compound to a lognormal annual return with exactly the category's mean and volatility (σ_m = √(ln(1 + σ²/(1+μ)²) / 12), μ_m = ln(1+μ)/12 − σ_m²/2), categories drawn **independently**, each from its own stream (so a scenario adding a category changes no other's draws); then it receives its part of the savings or gives its part of the withdrawals, never falling below zero; then the debts are repaid. Net worth is the assets minus the debts, Shortfall included. 5,000 paths, from a **fixed seed** shown on the page: the same assumptions, seed and engine version always give the same result, and every scenario uses the same draws, so their differences come from the assumptions only. Amounts are shown in **today's euros** (deflated by the inflation) by default, with a toggle for current euros. The fan is narrower than reality, which the page says: categories drawn independently, as if stocks, private equity and crypto never fell together (in a crash they do); returns with no crashes beyond the lognormal and no total loss (a failed platform or token); a fixed inflation; a Shortfall borrowed at a fixed 8 %; no tax on gains; savings ceilings (€22,950 on a Livret A, €12,000 on an LDDS) not modelled.
- F-SIM-5: **The projection page**, the wealth space's *Projection* tab (§7), a **simulator** in the app (React, its charts interactive: hovering a month shows every series' value that month), which recomputes as the settings change, without saving them. Its first card has two columns (one above the other on a phone):
  - the **settings**, left: the monthly contributions (F-SIM-3), one row each with its category, its amount a month and its category's return, a row to add (a category not yet used) and one to remove, the total a month and its share of the measured capacity; the horizon (1 to 40 years); the scope (*household*, *mine*, *joint*, as on the wealth page) and the euros (today's or current). **Save** keeps the contributions and the returns changed with them, **Cancel** goes back to the saved ones; until then the page says they are not saved, and every chart already shows them;
  - the **answer**, right: the median net worth at the horizon in large, its 10–90 % range under it, and **an income**: the **liquid** holdings at the horizon (every category but Real estate, Equipment, Private equity and Exotic, F-ANA-1: a home pays no income) × a **withdrawal rate** / 12 a month, in half of the outcomes (the median) and in nine in ten (the 10th percentile), the rate set on the page (4 % by default, from 2 % to 5 %, not saved): "About €X a month in half of the outcomes, €Y in nine in ten, at a 4 % withdrawal rate. A rule of thumb from US history, for holdings mostly in shares paying out over about 30 years: lower for a longer drawdown (3 to 3.5 % over 40 years or more) or for holdings mostly in savings accounts; before tax and social charges on the gains withdrawn; loan payments still running then not deducted. Not a promise: the retirement page (§4.13) works it out."; chips name what makes up the median: **today**, **contributions**, **debts repaid** and **gains**; then one chart with two views: **where the money comes from**, stacked areas over the horizon: today's net worth; the contributions added up (the monthly savings put in, freed debt payments included; without contributions, the savings added up, withdrawals subtracted); the debts repaid (the capital the debt lines repaid from the income, outside the contributions: what they owed at the start minus what they owe, Shortfall left out; the interest paid is not shown); and the gains, the median net worth minus the other three, below zero as losses when negative (the Shortfall's interest falls in them). In today's euros each amount counts at its value in today's euros when it was added (today's net worth as it is, each month's contribution and capital repaid divided by (1 + inflation)^(t/12)), so the gains are **after inflation**, negative when the holdings earn less than prices rise, and the band says so; in current euros, the amounts as paid and the gains before inflation. The first three are the same in every path, so the gains are exactly the median of the paths' gains and the four add up to the median every month; and **the range**, the fan below; under the chart: net worth today, contributed by the horizon, gains and debts repaid.
  Then:
  - the **fan** (the chart's second view): the median net worth over the horizon, with the 25–75 % and 10–90 % bands, today's value at its origin, the amount to reach as a line with the months half and nine tenths of the outcomes reach it marked on it, and under it: "An estimate from the assumptions below, not a forecast and not advice. One outcome in five lies outside the 10–90 % band, and the real spread is wider (see the limits). The median line is not one path: no single outcome follows it. Past returns do not guarantee future ones.";
  - **reaching an amount**, in the euros shown (the toggle converts it, so the chances do not change): given an amount and a month, the chance that net worth is at least that amount in that month ("In 72 % of the outcomes, net worth is at least €300,000 in December 2040"), and the months by which half and nine tenths of the outcomes have reached it once ("Half of the outcomes have reached it once by March 2037, nine tenths by…", "not within the horizon" otherwise); the share of outcomes that run into a Shortfall, when any does, next to the fan and the composition: "In N % of the outcomes the liquid holdings run out: the net worth still counts the home and locked-in funds, but the spending would have to be borrowed.";
  - the **composition**: each category's median today and at the horizon, side by side in the categories' colours (debts and Shortfall below zero), each category's own median, which together need not add up to the median net worth: the chart says so; its table gives every category month by month;
  - **scenarios**: the saved assumptions are the *base*; up to two **what-ifs** change the monthly savings (with contributions, their total, which scales each of them and stays above zero), a category's return or volatility (the other keeps its value), or the inflation, for this visit only (a different split is tried by changing the contributions themselves, which the page runs before saving them) (nothing is saved but from the assumptions form); the fan shows the base, the what-ifs their median, and a chart compares them at the horizon, each scenario's median as a point on its 10–90 % range, with its table (median, 10 % and 90 %, chance of reaching the amount).
  Chances are shown to the whole percent ("less than 1 %" and "more than 99 %" rather than 0 and 100), amounts to the nearest €100. Every chart has its table; folded at the end, the **advanced assumptions** (the inflation, each category's return and volatility with its median growth, the debts' terms, F-SIM-1), the method (paths, seed, engine version) and the limits of F-SIM-4.

### 4.12 Goals analysis and points to look at (phase 5)

The Goals page (F-GOAL-7) shows each goal on its own. The goals space's **Analysis** tab shows them **together**: against the savings capacity, in time, by risk and by where their money sits, and lists **points to look at**, observations computed by fixed rules from the entries and the projection assumptions (F-SIM-1). They are never advice: they state facts and the arithmetic, then options introduced by "consider" or "one option is", never an imperative (buy, sell, invest), and they name wealth categories only, never a provider, a fund or a contract. Everything below is computed, nothing is moved.

- F-ANA-1: **Common ground**.
  - The **reference month** is the last complete month in which every line funding an open goal of the scope has a value **entered** (not carried, F-WLT-3); the current month once all its values are entered. Every view and rule judges goals at the reference month, so a month not yet entered never shows as a month without gain; a goal started since is judged at its start month, on the values carried to it. The views against the capacity (F-ANA-2, R1) take the household's reference month, over the lines of the household's goals. The tab says which month it is.
  - **Volatile**: a wealth category whose volatility (F-SIM-1) is above 5 % or whose return is below 0. **Illiquid**: Real estate, Private equity, Equipment and Exotic, and categories added later that the household marks so (default: liquid).
  - A goal's **weights** on its funding lines: each envelope's amount at the reference month over the goal's value; for a goal holding nothing yet, its shares of the rises, normalised. Its **weighted volatility** σ_g = Σ wᵢσᵢ and **weighted real return** ρ_g = Σ wᵢμᵢ − inflation, μᵢ and σᵢ being the line's category's assumptions and inflation the household's (F-SIM-1; until the household's assumptions exist, the defaults of F-SIM-1 for the default categories, known as such whatever their name or colour becomes, and 0 % ± 0 for a category added later; the inflation is then 2 %). σ_g is an upper bound of the envelope's volatility (as if its holdings fell together, which they tend to do in a crash), the stance a goal calls for, and the tab says so. Its **loss in one outcome in ten**: with μ_g = Σ wᵢμᵢ and σ_g, the 10th percentile of a lump sum over the months left, by the lognormal of F-SIM-4.
  - Goal gains include the lines' returns (F-GOAL-3): on a volatile line, the actual effort (F-GOAL-5) and the statuses partly measure the market, not the household's saving. Rules on them say so when σ_g > 5 %.
  - The **capacity** (F-GOAL-6) is the household's: views and rules that compare goals with it always take the **household's goals**, in every scope, and say so in *mine* and *joint*. They need at least **3 complete months** of spending; below that they show "capacity measured over N months: too short to judge" instead, and from 3 to 11 months they add the F-SIM-3 sentence on the year's irregular costs.
  - Targets are in **current euros** (F-GOAL-1), so every figure here is too; rule R10 says what a long goal's target is worth after inflation.
  - Scope and visibility as the Goals page (§3): only the goals the viewer sees.
- F-ANA-2: **Required effort against capacity**: over the next 24 months (12 to 60), each goal with a deadline as a band of its **required effort** (F-GOAL-5), constant from the reference month to the month before its deadline (a goal due in the reference month for that month), then 0 (if each goal receives exactly its required effort every month, that effort does not change, so this curve is exact, not a guess), stacked by deadline, the nearest at the bottom; under them, what the plan gives the precaution (F-GOAL-6) over the months it takes to fill, in the foundation's ochre. The capacity is a flat line labelled "measured over N months". Months where the stack is above it are shaded **over-committed**, and the chart states their number and the largest excess. Pleasure goals and goals without a deadline are not drawn. Required efforts ignore the lines' returns, which makes them prudent for long goals (at 5 % a year in real terms, a 10-year goal needs about 22 % less each month): the chart says so.
- F-ANA-3: **Timeline**: one bar per open goal from its start to its deadline (an open-ended bar for a goal without one), in its tier's colour, with two markers: the **estimated date** at the actual effort (F-GOAL-5; an arrow past the edge for "never at this pace"; hollow, "not enough history", with fewer than 3 complete months) and the **date at the plan**, its remaining amount over its planned allocation (F-GOAL-6), the pace the household controls (the plan of the scope, as the Goals page shows it). A vertical line marks the reference month. Pleasure goals show their estimated dates only.
- F-ANA-4: **Horizon and risk**: each funded open goal as a point, months to its deadline across (0 to 120, the last tick "120+"; the precaution at 0, judged by R5 only), its weighted volatility σ_g up. Zones:
  - **red, short and volatile**: 24 months or fewer and σ_g above 5 %, or more than 10 % on volatile or illiquid lines (as R3 judges it);
  - **amber, glide path**: 25 to 60 months and the goal's volatile share above (months left − 24) / 36 (100 % allowed at 60 months, none at 24);
  - **red, long and eroding**: over 96 months and ρ_g at or below 0;
  - a goal more than 10 % in a category losing value (return below 0) is ringed **amber, losing value**, whatever its horizon.
  Each point says its goal, months left, σ_g, ρ_g and "in one outcome in ten, the envelope is worth X % less at the deadline". Goals without a deadline and pleasure goals are listed under the chart with σ_g and ρ_g.
- F-ANA-5: **Where the goals' money sits**: a Sankey from categories to lines to the goals' envelopes and each line's **free** part (F-GOAL-3), at the reference month, every line's flows adding up to its value. By default the liquid categories only (Real estate and Equipment left out, a toggle shows them), labelled "gross assets, debts not shown". "Free" may hold envelopes of goals the viewer does not see; the chart says so.
- F-ANA-6: **Status by month**: a grid of the open goals (rows, in the pyramid's order) by month (columns, from each goal's start to the reference month), each cell its status that month (F-GOAL-4) with its colour and icon, computed with today's targets and deadlines (edits are not versioned; the grid says so).
- F-ANA-7: **Chance of reaching each goal**: for each open goal with a deadline but the precaution and pleasure goals, the chance that its envelope reaches its target **at least once by its deadline**, by the engine of F-SIM-4 (same seed and engine version, categories independent, current euros, 5,000 paths): the envelope is held as its own amount on each funding line, growing with that line's category draws (the free part's cushion ignored, a prudent choice stated); each month it receives the goal's **planned allocation** (F-GOAL-6), split across its lines by their shares of the rises, normalised, until the target is reached; a second figure gives the chance **at the required effort**. The actual effort is never used: it already holds past returns, which the draws would count twice. A goal with σ_g = 0 shows "reached at this effort" or "short by €X at this effort" rather than a chance. The precaution shows the months to fill instead. Chances to the whole percent as F-SIM-5; "chances are per goal, not of reaching them all: a crash hits every goal on the same lines". Computed on the server and shown in the tab with the other views.
- F-ANA-8: **Points to look at**, at the top of the tab, and their count on the Goals page with a link. Each has a **severity** (warning, information or good news), the fact with its figures, why it matters, and options, each with a link where the app can do it (the goal's form prefilled, its envelopes, "Update this month"). Fixed thresholds, in the household's goals of the scope unless said:
  - **R0, data to update**: lines funding an open goal have no value entered for the last complete month (one point naming them; a line opened since is not waited for), or the capacity rests on fewer than 3 complete months. Warning, always first; it silences R2, R7 and R9. Option: "Update this month".
  - **R1, over-committed** (household's goals): the required efforts of the goals with a deadline, plus what the plan gives the precaution while it fills (F-GOAL-6), add up to more than the capacity (the stack of F-ANA-2 at the reference month), by an excess E above the greater of €20 and 5 % of the capacity. Warning; information instead when the excess only lasts while the precaution fills, that is, when the other goals still fit once it is full (their remaining amounts, less what the plan gives them until then, over their months left then, add up to no more than the capacity), with the months it takes. Options: for the goals in the order of F-GOAL-3, **farthest deadline first**, the first one for which postponing alone fits, within 60 months and 40 years from now: "postpone *Goal* by k months" with k = ⌈Rᵢ / (rᵢ − E)⌉ − nᵢ (Rᵢ its remaining amount, nᵢ its months left, rᵢ its required effort; postponing it alone cannot fit when E ≥ rᵢ), and the tier it would move to if it changes; "or lower its target by E × nᵢ"; the other goals for which postponing fits under "other options"; none: "postponing one goal is not enough: €E a month is missing".
  - **R2, behind for three months**: the goal is behind (F-GOAL-4) at each of the last 3 reference months by at least the greater of €50 and one month of its required effort. Warning when its deadline is 12 months away or less, information otherwise; it clears after 2 months in a row not behind. Options: "+€x a month keeps the date" (the extra effort of F-GOAL-5), "or the date moves to *estimated date*", "or the target to *what the actual effort reaches by the deadline*"; with σ_g above 5 %: "part of this gap may come from market moves on *categories*".
  - **R3, short goal on volatile lines**: months left 24 or fewer, more than 10 % of the goal's value on volatile or illiquid lines and that part above €500: warning; months left 25 to 60 and that share above (months left − 24) / 36: information. The fact gives the loss in one outcome in ten in euros. Option: "consider funding it from lines with little volatility as the deadline nears". The precaution is left to R5, and pleasure goals, without a deadline, are not judged here (nor by R4 and R10).
  - **R3b, losing value**: a category whose return is below 0 holds more than 10 % of a goal. Information.
  - **R4, long goal eroding**: months left above 96, ρ_g at or below 0, and value plus remaining amount at least €5,000; not the precaution. Information only. The fact gives the erosion over the months left, V × (1 − ((1 + r) / (1 + i))^N) for the goal's value V, its weighted return r, the inflation i and N years (€10,000 over 10 years: €290 at 1.7 % against 2 %). Option, in categories only: "consider categories with a higher expected return (stocks, bonds), which trade certainty for expected return".
  - **R5, the precaution**: no precaution goal in the scope: warning, the first rule after R0; a target below 3 months of the average monthly fixed expenses (F-GOAL-2, recomputed today since the target never changes by itself): warning, from 3 to below 6 months: information, with "fixed expenses leave out groceries and other day-to-day spending; a single income or a self-employed one may want more"; any part of it on a volatile or illiquid line: warning; any part on a current account: information (available and guaranteed, but earning nothing and mixed with the spending).
  - **R6, ceiling near**: a savings line with a **regulated type** (F-WLT-2) worth at least its ceiling minus €500 and receiving rises for an open goal. Information: "deposits above the €x ceiling are refused; interest can still take it over". Ceilings as of October 2026: Livret A €22,950, LDDS €12,000, LEP €10,000, PEL €61,200, Livret jeune €1,600, kept in `kwak_core` (`kwak_core.wealth.CEILINGS`).
  - **R7, idle money**: the free part of the current accounts above the average monthly income plus the average monthly spending (the next month's salary sits there at the end of a month; with fewer than 3 months of history, 4 times the monthly fixed expenses). Warning while a goal is behind or the precaution falls short, information otherwise (it loses the inflation every year). Option: "consider setting €x aside for *Goal* (an adjustment of its envelope, F-GOAL-3, which moves no money), and moving it to a savings line".
  - **R8, the capstone within reach** (good news): shown once the precaution is reached and the capstone is locked (F-GOAL-10) by one goal ahead only: for each goal on track or behind, the one-off amount that makes it ahead (the fewest whole euros above expected value × (1 + band), or up to its target, reaching it counting as ahead), the smallest first, ties by nearest deadline, and the monthly alternative: what it would take each month, in all, to be ahead in 3 months; never from the precaution. It counts the scope's goals: in *mine* and *joint*, as the capstone does (F-GOAL-10); in *household*, everyone's together. While the precaution falls short: "precaution at x %: €y to go".
  - **R9, no gain**: a goal at least 6 months old, not reached, with no month of positive gain in its last 6 reference months. Warning when its deadline is 12 months away or less, information otherwise. Options: relaunch it (its required effort), postpone it, cancel it.
  - **R10, target not indexed**: months left above 60. Information: "€x of today is about €x × (1 + i)^N at the deadline, at the assumed inflation".
  - **R11, unfunded goal**: an open goal without a funding line, which can never progress. Warning. Option: its form.
  - **R12, costly debt** (once the debts' rates exist, F-SIM-1): a debt line whose rate is above the greater of 6 % and the best return assumed for a line funding a goal plus 1 point. Information: "it costs more than the goals' lines are assumed to earn".
  - **R13, debt payments** (once the debts' payments exist, F-SIM-1): the debt lines' monthly payments over the average monthly income above 35 %, the lenders' ceiling in France (HCSF, insurance included). Information, worth knowing before a home-deposit goal.
  - **R14, capacity left over** (good news, household's goals, with at least 3 complete months of spending): the plan leaves more than €50 unallocated (F-GOAL-6) and no goal is behind. Option: a new goal.
  At most **one point per goal** (its most severe), ordered by severity then R0, R5 (none, below 3 months, on volatile or illiquid lines), R11, R1, R3, R2, R9, R12, R5 (current account), R7, R6, R13, R4, R3b, R10, R8, R14, then the nearest deadline first; **three** shown, then "N more". Each member can **snooze** a point until the next reference month or **dismiss** it until it worsens (its severity rises, or its main amount grows by more than 25 %); the choice is the member's own; R0 and "no precaution goal" can be neither. Under the points: "Rule-based observations computed from your entries and the Projection assumptions. Not personalised investment advice nor a recommendation to buy or sell any product; for a decision that matters, ask a licensed adviser." There is no rule on the wealth's overall allocation (a crypto share, say), which would be portfolio advice beyond the goals.
- F-ANA-9: Every view has its table and reads its marks out in words, as F-WLT-6: no value reachable only by hovering. The views are React charts fed by the API, the chances of F-ANA-7 included.

### 4.13 Retirement (phase 5)

What standard of living the household could keep once retired: its **pensions** and what its **wealth** can pay each month, against what it would **spend**. It runs on the projection's engine and assumptions (§4.11) and, like it, is an estimate, never a forecast nor advice. It is not FIRE: nobody is assumed to stop working early; the household chooses when it retires.

- F-RET-1: **Each member's retirement**, entered by the member for themselves and seen by the household's members (the household's plan needs them), in today's euros:
  - the **birth month** (a month and a year, no day);
  - the **retirement month**, when the member stops working (this month or later; the page shows the member's age then), and the **net monthly earnings that stop then**, as they reach the account, after income tax withheld at source like the income lines (the app cannot split the household's income lines by member);
  - the **pension month** (the retirement month by default, or later: one who stops at 62 and claims at 64 lives two years on the wealth) and the expected **net monthly pension** from then, as it would reach the account: info-retraite.fr gives it after social charges and before income tax, so take off the expected withholding rate, as the income lines are after it; enter the estimate for the age at the pension month (the simulator gives one per age: claiming earlier lowers it), in today's euros (check the simulator's note, and deflate an amount given in euros of the departure year). The app computes no pension rights.

  A member without a birth month is left out and the page names them. Edited from the Retirement tab.
- F-RET-2: **The household's retirement**, settings any member can change, kept until changed:
  - the **retirement spending**, a share of today's average monthly spending **outside the debts' payments and borrower insurance** (F-SIM-1), 80 % by default, from 50 % to 150 %, or a monthly amount entered by hand, in today's euros: one spends less on work (transport, meals) and on the children, more on health (a complementary health insurance no longer shared by an employer, often €100 to €200 a month a person) and leisure; a tenant keeps paying rent. The average spending is the spending of F-DSH-4 (expenses only, savings lines left out) over the capacity's months (F-GOAL-6), with the same warning below 12 months;
  - the **pension indexation relative to inflation**, −0.3 % a year by default, from −2 % to +1 %: base pensions follow prices by law, supplementary ones (Agirc-Arrco) have often been raised by less, and were frozen from 2015 to 2018 and in November 2025;
  - the **horizon**, the age of the household's youngest member at which the plan ends, 95 by default, from 85 to 105, at most 80 years from now: at 60, about one French woman in four and one man in six or seven may reach 95 (INSEE, projected mortality); for a couple, the chance that one of them does is about one in three: planning to 95 is a margin, not a ceiling.
- F-RET-3: **The simulation**, the projection's engine (F-SIM-4: the same seed, draws, assumptions and allocation; a path's draws do not depend on the horizon, so its first months are the projection's), in the household scope (the wealth lines the viewer sees: the page says when a member's private lines are left out), from this month to the horizon, month by month:
  - the savings start as the capacity (F-SIM-3); from a member's retirement month, they lose that member's earnings; from their pension month, they gain their pension. A pension already received this month is in the capacity's income: it adds nothing, and that member's earnings are 0;
  - from the **household's retirement** (the last member's retirement month), the savings are the pensions started less the retirement spending, the pensions indexed to inflation plus the indexation setting, the spending to inflation;
  - the debts keep their repayments (F-SIM-1); before the household's retirement, a repaid debt frees its payment as in the projection; from then on, each debt's payment and insurance are spent on top of the retirement spending, in current euros, until it is repaid, and a repaid debt frees nothing (it simply stops being spent);
  - what is left over is saved by the allocation; what is missing is withdrawn as negative savings are (current accounts, then savings accounts, so the emergency fund first, then the other liquid categories in proportion; never the home, equipment, private equity or exotic holdings), then borrowed as the Shortfall. The home is kept, neither sold nor mortgaged.
- F-RET-4: **What the page shows**, the wealth space's *Retirement* tab (§7), an embedded Dash page as the projection, laid out as the analysis pages of §7: first the standard of living as a **tile** with the pensions and the withdrawal as one bar against a mark at the retirement spending, then tiles for the share covered and the chance that the wealth lasts; a **timeline** of the members, each one's working months then pension to the horizon, with today, each retirement and the horizon marked and labelled with the ages; then:
  - the **standard of living** at retirement, in today's euros: the pensions, plus the **sustainable withdrawal**, the largest multiple of €100 a month, indexed to inflation, that each outcome's liquid wealth at the household's retirement can pay alone (no pension nor spending, the debts' payments being in the spending) to the horizon without running into a Shortfall in 9 outcomes in 10 (and in half of them, given next to it); 9 in 10 in the model, not a guarantee: the real chance is lower (see the limits). Their sum against the retirement spending: "From 2047, pensions of €2,600 and a sustainable withdrawal of €1,100 a month give €3,700 a month in 9 outcomes in 10 (€4,900 in half of them), for spending of €3,400: 109 % covered." When a pension starts after the household's retirement, the years before it are paid from the same wealth: the chance that the wealth lasts counts them; the withdrawal does not. When more than one outcome in ten is already in Shortfall at the household's retirement, the withdrawal is "none";
  - the **chance that the wealth lasts**: the share of outcomes in which the plan of F-RET-3 runs to the horizon without a Shortfall, and the ages of the youngest member at which the liquid wealth runs out in 1 outcome in 10 and in half of them ("not before 95" otherwise);
  - a **fan** of net worth to the horizon (the projection's colours), the liquid part as its own median line, the household's retirement and each pension's start marked; under it, when outcomes run into a Shortfall: "from then on, the spending would have to be cut, or financed otherwise (the home, family)";
  - a **monthly income chart** from the retirement: the pensions stacked with the sustainable withdrawal, the retirement spending (debts' payments included while they run) as a line;
  - under the charts: "An estimate from the assumptions and the pensions you entered, not a forecast and not advice. Pension estimates change with your career and with reforms: check yours on info-retraite.fr. Past returns do not guarantee future ones.";
  - each chart's table, the inputs used (one card per member with birth, retirement month and age, earnings and pension; then the spending, indexation and horizon) and, folded, the **limits**: those of F-SIM-4; pensions as entered, never revised but by the indexation setting; no tax on withdrawals (from a PEA, a CTO or an assurance vie, the gains part is taxed, often 5 to 15 % of what is withdrawn after many years); long-term care not modelled (a nursing home costs about €2,000 to €2,500 a month after aid); the household's composition fixed: no death, separation nor inheritance (after a death, the survivor keeps a reversion pension, about 54 % of a base pension, means-tested, and 60 % of an Agirc-Arrco one, and spends about two thirds to three quarters of what a couple does, so both are overstated in such years); the home never sold.

  Amounts to the nearest €100 (the sustainable withdrawal rounded down), chances to the whole percent as F-SIM-5.

### 4.14 Cash-flow forecast (phase 5)

What the next twelve months of spending could look like: each category's income, spending or savings month by month, their totals and the rest, with a band of likely values, against the targets. The fixed expenses due are known in advance; the rest is forecast from the household's own history by **models chosen by back-testing**: each candidate forecasts months already entered, as if they were still to come, and each series keeps the one that erred least, a model never replacing the simple baseline unless it does clearly better. It is an estimate from past spending, never a forecast of decisions to come nor advice, and the page says so. The study behind these choices (statistical, machine-learning and foundation models compared, probabilistic forecasts, explainability, causal inference) is a set of notebooks run on synthetic data only (`notebooks/forecasting/`).

- F-FCT-1: **The series**: one per spending category (expense, savings or income, F-CAT-1), its amount per calendar month (F-LIN-2), split lines counting in each part's category, positive as the month's figures (F-DSH-4: income received, spent, saved net of withdrawals, so a savings series may go below zero). The **origin** is the last complete month, the one before the current month; the household's **history** runs from the first month holding a line to the origin, 60 months at most, and each category's from the first month holding one of its lines, a month without lines counting as 0 from then on (a category added late is not pulled down by the months before it). Nothing after the origin is read: the lines already entered in the current month are shown next to its forecast, never used by it. A category with no line in the history has no variable part: it is forecast at its fixed expenses due only, if any, which its row says.
- F-FCT-2: **Fixed and variable**: a line is **fixed** when it was created from a fixed expense (F-FIX-2), or when, typed by hand with the label (case and accents ignored) and the category of a fixed expense, it falls in a month where that fixed expense's schedule, extended back before its first month at its frequency, has an occurrence, one line per occurrence (the closest in amount), so a payment is never counted both as known and in what the models learn; split lines are matched only when created from one, and every other line is variable. Each series is forecast as its **known part**, the fixed expenses' occurrences due in each month to come (F-FIX-2: every first, third or twelfth month from the first one, up to the last; skipped occurrences left out, paused fixed expenses giving nothing until resumed), at their amount, plus its **variable part**, the series without its fixed lines, forecast by a model. Fixed expenses keep no history of their amounts: in the back-test, the known part of a past month is each matched occurrence at the fixed expense's **current amount**, so the gap to what was paid (an estimated bill, a rise) is part of the errors and of the bands. Income has no fixed expenses (F-CAT-1): its series are all variable.
- F-FCT-3: **The candidates**, forecasting each variable series from its own months up to a cut, from the simplest:
  - **baseline**: the average of the last 6 months (fewer when the series is shorter);
  - **averages**: of the last 3 months, of the last 12 and of the whole history;
  - from **13 months** of training at a cut, the others: the last month (naive), simple exponential smoothing, an automatically chosen exponential smoothing (ETS, seasonal at 12 months allowed), Theta, an automatically chosen ARIMA, ADIDA for series with many empty months (a gift, a repair), and with **24 months** of the series' history, the same month last year (seasonal naive); then **machine learning**: gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM), one model for all the household's categories, on each series' last months (1, 2, 3, 6 and 12 months before), their 3- and 6-month averages, the calendar month and the category, each series scaled by its own mean and spread, trained with a fixed seed and forecasting month after month; and, in an image built with them (F-FCT-7), two **foundation models**, networks pretrained on many public series that forecast a series from its own months alone, without being trained on it (zero-shot): **Chronos-2** (Amazon, 120 million parameters) and **TimesFM 2.5** (Google, 200 million), each forecasting every series from its months up to the cut (60 at most), their **mean** taken as the forecast (TimesFM's own mean; for Chronos-2, which gives none, the average of its deciles 0.1 to 0.9; as the averages, so that sums over months and categories add up; their median forecast about 10 % less spending than there was in the study), run on the processor in 32-bit floats, with a fixed number of threads and batch size.
  The order above, baseline, averages, naive, seasonal naive, exponential smoothing, ETS, Theta, ARIMA, ADIDA, trees, Chronos-2, TimesFM 2.5, is the order of simplicity. The study (§4.14's notebooks) found that keeping the models for 24 months of history cost accuracy, the 10 % margin being guard enough, and that the two foundation models were the most accurate of all on its synthetic households; the average of their quantiles forecast between 2 % more and 3 % less spending than there was, against about 1 % less for the 6-month average, and the rule against optimism (F-FCT-4) applies to them as to every candidate. Their own quantiles are not used: the bands stay the back-test's (F-FCT-5), as for every candidate. Neural networks trained on the household and a cloud model (TimeGPT, which would send the data off the host, §6) are left out.
- F-FCT-4: **The back-test**: the household's history is cut at every month from the 13th (the 7th below 24 months of history) to the one before the origin; at each cut, every candidate is trained on the months up to it only and forecasts the next 12 months, never past the origin (a category not yet started at a cut is forecast at 0 there, which is what it was). Errors are measured in each series by the **MASE** (mean absolute error over the mean absolute month-to-month change of the training months), with the **WAPE** (absolute errors over absolute amounts) and the **RMSSE** (the squared counterpart of the MASE) shown beside it. Each candidate is judged against the baseline on the same cuts: the series' own cuts (those where its history is long enough) that it covers, 6 at least (all of them when there are fewer), since a model needing 13 months of training misses the first cuts of a short history. Each variable series keeps the candidate whose MASE is lowest relative to the baseline's; it replaces the baseline only when that MASE is **at least 10 % lower**, ties going to the simpler. A candidate is **never more optimistic than the baseline**: its **bias** on a series is the sum of its errors (entered − forecast) over the sum of the absolute amounts entered, both of its variable part (F-FCT-2), both sums over every cut and number of months ahead its MASE is measured on, each forecast to the cent. It is **optimistic** when positive for an expense (less spending forecast than entered), when negative for an income (more forecast than received), and either way for a savings category (more saved forecast reads as on track; less inflates the rest). A candidate whose optimistic bias is above **5 %** of the absolute amounts entered and above **€10 a month** on average (the sum of its errors over the number of months counted), and above the baseline's on the same months, is **set aside before the choice**, however low its MASE (an optimistic forecast reads as room in the budget that is not there): the series keeps the best of the others. The baseline is never set aside; without any amount entered in those months, a series has no bias and nothing is set aside. A series keeps the baseline without a contest when fewer than 3 of its months hold a line, when it has no cut of its own, or when a training at one of its cuts never changed from month to month (its MASE is undefined). A forecast needs **12 complete months** of history; below that the page says how many months there are and points to the demo (F-ADM-5).
- F-FCT-5: **The band**: around each forecast, the **80 % band** comes from the back-test's own errors at the same number of months ahead (a conformal interval: no distribution assumed): the chosen candidate's errors for a category, the errors of the summed forecasts for a total, so the categories' errors that offset or add up are kept. Of n errors sorted, the band runs from the ⌈(n+1) × 0.1⌉-th smallest to the ⌈(n+1) × 0.9⌉-th, and needs **n ≥ 9**; a number of months ahead with fewer than 9 errors takes the band of the longest one with 9 or more, widened by √(h / h₀) (h months ahead, h₀ the longest with enough), and the page says from which month it is widened; without 9 errors one month ahead, there is no band. The band always holds its forecast (from the forecast plus the lesser of that low error and 0 to the forecast plus the greater of that high error and 0), and a spending or income forecast and its band never go below zero. A **sum over k months** (the summary's twelve months, the months to December) takes its band from the back-test's errors of the same k-month sums, never by adding monthly bands, and shows none without 9 of them. The back-test's errors overlap from one cut to the next and the past months may not resemble the coming ones: 80 % is approximate, and the page shows how often it held. The **chance** of going above an amount is (k + 1) / (n + 1), k of the n errors carrying the forecast above it, shown to the nearest 5 % ("rare in the past months" below 5 %, "nearly always in the past months" above 95 %), as is the chance of falling below. Each forecast is its known part plus its variable part; the month's income, spent and saved are the sums of their categories' forecasts and the rest is income − spent − saved (F-DSH-4), each total with its own band.
- F-FCT-6: **The page**, the Spending space's *Forecast* tab (§7), an embedded Dash page in the app's theme and language, for the household (spending is the household's, §3), laid out as the analysis pages of §7:
  - a **summary**, as tiles (the rest over the 12 months with its band, the current month's spending with a bar against its forecast and a mark at the share of the month gone, the targets at risk this month and this year, and the income, spending and savings over the 12 months), the rest's warning above them, each tile's sentence under its figure: "From October 2026 to September 2027, an income of €X, spending of €Y and savings of €Z are expected, leaving a rest of €R (between €A and €B about 8 times in 10)."; and for the current month, what is already entered against its forecast ("€1,240 spent so far, €2,950 expected for the month", or "already above what was expected" once past the band's top);
  - **month by month**: one chart, the last 12 months as entered then the 12 to come (the current month first) as forecast with their bands, income and spent as lines, the fixed part of the spending as bars, and under them, on the same months, the rest as bars (forecast ones lighter, with their band); its table gives the last 24 months and the 12 to come, with saved;
  - **against the targets**: each expense category with a target in force (F-BUD-1) in the current month, the first forecast: its forecast and band, the target and the **chance of going over** it, as **likely over** (a chance of 50 % or more), **may go over** (20 % or more) or **within**, the likeliest overrun first, drawn as a row per category in percent of its target (the forecast a point, the band a bar, the target a line at 100 %); then, for the current year, each target's spending to date (to the origin) plus its forecast to December against the full-year target (F-BUD-10), with its band and chance, drawn as a bar per category in percent of its target, the part to date solid and the forecast lighter, the band at its end; the savings categories against their objectives (F-BUD-9) the same way, with the chance of falling **below** them (**likely short**, **may fall short**, **on track**); the income categories with their forecast and band only, as bars;
  - folded under **how reliable it is**, what the forecast rests on and the back-test: **what the forecast rests on**, per category, drawn as how much less its method errs than the baseline and its bias, then its table: its chosen method in words ("the average of the last 3 months", "the same month last year", "exponential smoothing weighing the last months most", "trees learnt on all the categories, mostly from the last month and the calendar month", "Chronos-2, a model pretrained on many public series, from this category's months alone"), its known part, its MASE and WAPE against the baseline's ("errs 18 % less than the 6-month average"), its bias in the past months tested ("forecast 3 % less spending than was entered", "3 % more income than was received", "3 % more savings than was saved", "about what was entered" under 1 %), the candidate set aside for its optimism if it erred less than the one kept ("TimesFM 2.5 erred 12 % less but forecast 7 % less spending than was entered in the past months tested, so it was not used"), and how often the actual months fell inside the band in the back-test, each band built only from the errors known at its cut (80 % expected); for the trees, the three features that weighed most in its forecast (their contributions, by TreeSHAP);
  - **the back-test**: the totals forecast one month ahead at each cut against what was entered, with their MASE, WAPE, RMSSE and **bias** (from the summed back-test errors of F-FCT-5: "spending forecast 3 % below what was entered, the rest €90 a month above"); when the rest's forecast was above what was left by more than 2 % of the income and €10 a month on average, the summary adds "In the past months tested, this forecast put the rest €90 a month above what was left: the rest may be lower than shown.";
  - under the charts: "An estimate drawn from your past months: not a prediction of your decisions, and not advice. A new expense, a change of job or a move are not in the history: the band reflects only the past months' surprises, and holds about 8 times in 10 if the coming months resemble them." and the **limits**: a fixed expense counted at its current amount; the bands rest on the back-tests that chose the methods, so they may be narrow, which the measured coverage shows; under 24 months, a sum that comes once a year (a 13th month, a bonus, an annual tax or insurance) is forecast only if it is a fixed expense, otherwise it is spread over the months; amounts in current euros, not deflated; a price rise faster than in the past months is forecast late, so in such a period the spending forecast may be low.
  Amounts to the nearest euro. Every chart has its table; every table sits under its chart, folded. The forecast is computed when the page opens (a few seconds), with a fixed seed and engine version shown on it, and whether the foundation models took part (with their versions) or are not installed (F-FCT-7).
- F-FCT-7: **The foundation models are optional**, off by default: they need a deep-learning runtime (PyTorch, for the processor only) and their weights, about 2.5 GB more in the API's image. A setting in `.env` builds the image with them; the weights are downloaded **when the image is built**, at a pinned revision whose files are checked against their recorded checksums (the build fails otherwise), and the API reads them offline: nothing is downloaded nor sent when a forecast runs (§6). Without them the candidates are those of F-FCT-3 up to the trees, and the forecast is the same as an image built without the setting gives. On the same host, the same lines, seed, engine version and model revisions give the same forecast (§8); PyTorch's results may differ in their last digits from one processor to another.

| 5 — Analytics & DS | First the **wealth projection** (§4.11: Monte Carlo from today's wealth, the savings and assumptions by category, allocation scenarios), then the **goals analysis** (§4.12: required effort against capacity, timeline, horizon and risk, where the goals' money sits, status by month, the chance of reaching each goal, and rule-based points to look at), then **retirement** on the same engine (§4.13: the standard of living pensions and wealth give against the spending; not FIRE), then the cash-flow **forecast** (§4.14: the next twelve months by category, fixed expenses known, statistical, machine-learning and, optionally, foundation models chosen per series by back-testing against a naive baseline, never more optimistic than it, conformal bands, against the targets; the study in notebooks on synthetic data). Interactive Dash pages for exploration |
| 6 — Local GenAI | Ollama-based assistant: natural-language questions over your own data (text-to-SQL on a read-only analytics view), categorisation suggestions, monthly narrative summary. No data leaves the host |

## 6. Non-functional requirements

- **Privacy**: all data stays on the host. No telemetry. Model weights (the forecast's optional foundation models, F-FCT-7) are downloaded only when the image is built, never at run time. Real financial files are never committed to Git (`data/` is ignored; test fixtures are synthetic or anonymised).
- **Correctness of money**: amounts use `Decimal` / `NUMERIC`, never floats. Sign convention: negative = outflow (the grid shows expenses as positive amounts, F-LIN-3). Every aggregate is reproducible from the lines and the wealth values. The exceptions are the Monte Carlo engine (F-SIM-4), which draws its paths in floating point for speed, and the forecasting engine (F-FCT-3), whose models fit in floating point: their inputs are `Decimal` and their results are rounded back to `Decimal` cents before they are shown or compared, and their inputs, seed and engine version are kept with them.
- **Security**: 2FA, argon2id, CSRF protection on cookie sessions, secrets in `.env` (not committed), dependency audit in CI. The app is served on the LAN behind a reverse proxy with TLS.
- **Performance**: the app should feel responsive on a home server with about 100k lines (lists < 300 ms, dashboard < 1 s).
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AA contrast in light and dark themes, keyboard navigation.
- **Quality**: test-first development. Property-based tests for financial invariants (see §8). CI must pass before any merge to `main`.
- **Reproducibility**: locked dependencies (`uv.lock`, `pnpm-lock.yaml`), versioned migrations, fixed and logged random seeds for simulations and forecasts.

Wealth category colours (F-WLT-1) are a categorical set of semantic tokens, `wealth-*`, one per default top-level category, for both themes: earthy hues in the blog palette's spirit, all at one chroma level in OKLCH and alternating light and dark along the matrix order so neighbours stay apart: a forest green (savings accounts), a sage (current accounts), a denim blue (stocks), a heather (crypto), a petrol blue (bonds), a dusty rose (private equity), an ochre (gold), a rust (real estate), a periwinkle (equipment), a sea green (exotic) and a brick for debts. Each category's colour marks its row (a swatch) and its lines (a side bar). Ten hues cannot all stay apart for every colour-vision deficiency, so colour is never the only cue: every category keeps its label in the matrix and the charts, a legend in the same order as the stack, and its table. The tokens sit in a `@theme static` block: the page reads them through `var()` only, and Tailwind would otherwise drop them from the build.

## 8. Domain invariants (to be enforced by tests)

Numbers are kept stable: an invariant retired with v1.0 keeps its number.

1. *(retired in v1.0: no accounts)*
2. The parts of a split line sum exactly to the line, each positive; summed over categories, a split line counts its amount exactly once.
3. *(retired in v1.0: no transfers)*
4. *(retired in v1.0: no import)*
5. A wealth line's value in a month is the last value entered in that month or before (carried forward), and nothing before its first value or after its closing.
6. A month's net worth equals the sum of its asset lines minus the sum of its debt lines; each category's subtotal is the sum of its lines, and the scopes add up as in invariant 11 (*household* = *mine* + *joint* + the lines other members share).
7. Forecasts and back-tests (phase 5) never read data after their origin date.
8. A category has at most one target (or objective) in force in any month: the one set from the latest month not after it.
9. A year's target to date equals the sum of the monthly targets in force from January to the month shown, and its full-year target the sum over the twelve months.
10. The matrix, the month so far, the year and the reports never disagree: a category's spending in a month is the same everywhere, and a year's or a period's is the sum of its months'.
11. The *mine* and *joint* scopes of the wealth and the goals never share a line, and the *household* scope is exactly *mine*, plus *joint*, plus the lines other members share.
12. *(retired in v1.0: no transfers)*
13. Money flows balance: what comes in (income, taken from savings, deficit) equals what goes out (spending, saved, unallocated).
14. Savings lines are never spending: a month's rest equals the sum of its lines, income, expense and savings alike (income positive, the others negative); its income is the sum of its income lines (and income parts) and its spending the sum of its expense lines (and expense parts) only.
15. *(retired in v1.0: no budget month; every line counts in the month of its date)*
16. A line fulfils at most one fixed-expense occurrence and an occurrence at most one line; the projected rest equals the rest minus the amounts of the occurrences of the month neither confirmed nor skipped.
17. *(retired in v1.0: no import)*
18. A report never disagrees with the home page: its income, spending and savings over a period equal the sums of the KPIs of its months (F-DSH-4), and the category lines of each section add up to the section's total.
19. A line's shares of the rises across open goals never exceed 100 %. In every month, on every line, the goals' envelopes are never negative and together never exceed the line's value (nor zero when it has none); no goal holds more than its target; and a fall that leaves the line worth at least what its goals held changes none of them.
20. The effort plan never hands out more than the capacity and no goal more than it needs (its gap for the precaution, its required effort otherwise); while a precaution goal falls short, the precaution receives the least of its gap and its share of the capacity before any other goal.
21. The quick line loses no word: every word typed ends up in exactly one of the date, the amount, the category word or the label.
22. A pleasure goal never gains from a line's rise in a month it is locked, and the effort plan gives it nothing then; when unlocked, the plan gives the pleasure goals only what is left once every other goal has what it needs and the precaution its gap, never more than each still lacks.
23. A projection never reads a wealth value after its starting month, and its first month is exactly the starting point: each category's total and the net worth of the scope that month (invariant 6).
24. With every volatility at 0, every path is the same and equals the month-by-month compounding of the returns, savings and repayments, so all percentiles coincide; the same assumptions, seed and engine version give the same paths.
25. In every month of every path, no asset category is below zero, no debt below zero, and a debt line whose payment covers its interest never above its starting amount; the percentiles are ordered (10 % ≤ 25 % ≤ median ≤ 75 % ≤ 90 %); and the chance of reaching an amount never rises when the amount does.
26. With every volatility at 0, a category at return r grows by exactly 1 + r over 12 months; over many seeded paths, the mean of each category's simulated annual growth equals its return and its standard deviation its volatility, within the Monte Carlo error.
27. Money is conserved: in every month of every path, the change in gross assets equals the returns plus the savings added minus the withdrawals, and a withdrawal never takes more than the assets hold; what is missing becomes the Shortfall debt, never lost, which grows by its interest until repaid. A debt falls each month by its payment minus its interest, never below zero.
28. An amount in today's euros at month t equals the amount in current euros divided by (1 + inflation)^(t/12); switching the unit never changes a chance or a month reached.
29. The month by which half the outcomes have reached an amount once is never after the month for nine tenths; in every month, the share of outcomes that have reached an amount once is at least the chance of being at or above it then.
30. The goals analysis reads no wealth value after its reference month (but for a goal started since, judged at its start month), and every line funding an open goal of the scope has a value entered in it; a status, gain or effort there equals the one of F-GOAL-4 and F-GOAL-5 computed at that month.
31. A goal with a deadline that receives exactly its required effort every month keeps the same required effort until its deadline: the forward bands of F-ANA-2 are constant, and their stack never rises from one month to the next.
32. The postponement R1 proposes makes the required efforts, with what the plan gives the precaution while it fills, fit within the capacity, and one month less does not; lowering the target by the amount it gives fits too.
33. A goal's weights on its lines are positive and sum to 1, and its weighted volatility lies between the smallest and the largest of its lines' categories' volatilities.
34. In the Sankey, each line's flows (its envelopes and its free part) add up to its value at the reference month, and each goal's flows, with what it holds on the lines left out (real estate and equipment by default), to its value.
35. A goal's chance of being reached never falls when its planned allocation rises nor when its target falls; with every volatility at 0, it is 0 or 1 and matches compounding the allocation month by month.
36. At most one point to look at per goal; a dismissed point comes back exactly when its severity rises or its main amount grows by more than 25 %; R0 and "no precaution goal" are never hidden.
37. Until the first member's retirement month, a retirement simulation's paths are exactly the projection's in the household scope with the same inputs (same seed, draws and savings); a path's draws do not depend on the horizon.
38. The sustainable withdrawal never falls when a pension, a member's earnings or an asset category's starting value rises, nor when the horizon comes nearer; with every volatility at 0, it never falls when a return rises. It is the largest multiple of €100 at which no more than one outcome in ten runs into a Shortfall between the household's retirement and the horizon; at €100 more, more than one does. Paths already in Shortfall at the retirement count as failing; when more than one in ten are, it is "none".
39. With every volatility at 0, the chance that the wealth lasts is 0 or 100 %, and the sustainable withdrawal equals the constant real payment that empties the liquid wealth exactly at the horizon (rounded down to €100).
40. The demo and the real household never meet: no answer given to a session in the demo reads or writes a row of the real household, none given outside it a row of the demo; resetting the demo changes no row outside it; the same seed and day generate the same demo, which satisfies every invariant above.

41. A forecast reads no line dated after its origin, and each back-test cut trains on the months up to it only; on the same host, the same lines, fixed expenses, seed, engine version and foundation models' revisions (or their absence) give the same forecast.
42. Forecasts add up: each category's forecast is its known part plus its variable part; a month's income, spent and saved are the sums of their categories' and its rest is income − spent − saved; a line is in a variable series or matched to one fixed occurrence, never both, and an occurrence takes one line at most.
43. Bands hold their forecast (low ≤ forecast ≤ high); an expense or income forecast and its band are never below zero; a series holding one constant amount every month is forecast at that amount with a band of zero width; a chance of going above an amount never rises when the amount does; a band needs 9 errors, and its coverage in the back-test is measured only with bands built from the errors known at each cut.
44. Without 12 complete months of history there is no forecast; a series keeps a candidate other than the baseline only when its back-test MASE is at least 10 % below the baseline's on the same cuts, and the same month last year only from 24 months of its history; a candidate whose back-test bias on a series is optimistic (an expense under-forecast, an income over-forecast, a savings category either way) by more than 5 % of the absolute amounts entered and €10 a month, and more than the baseline's on the same months, is never kept; the baseline is never set aside.
45. With monthly contributions, the monthly savings are their sum (each indexed or fixed as set) and the allocation their split, and a category without a contribution receives no savings; without any, the projection is the one from the capacity and the default allocation.
46. In every month, today's net worth, the contributions added up and the debts repaid being the same in every path, the gains equal the median over the paths of their net worth minus those three, so the four add up to the median net worth, in the euros shown. The parts are converted month by month as they are added, so unlike the totals (invariant 28), a part in today's euros is not its current-euros value deflated at the horizon.
